Every healthcare data tool ships with a test corpus. Ours did too — thousands of fixtures, every one hand-constructed to be *correct*. That validation is real, but it has a structural blind spot: clean synthetic data only exercises the cases its authors imagined.

This post is about what happened when we pointed the stack at real hospital data: what genuine ICU content does to resource deduplication, and a before/after experiment where an auto-coder had to reconstruct stripped clinical codes from free text — graded against the codes we removed.

## The problem with clean test data

fhir4ds ships a data-quality stack: resource deduplication (temporal clustering over coded clinical events), PIQI scorecards (pass/fail data-quality assessments per data class), and an auto-coder that augments text-only `CodeableConcepts` with terminology codes via medterm4ds. All of it was validated against synthetic corpora — CMS eCQM fixtures and our own constructed cases.

That validation is real, but it has a structural blind spot: **synthetic data is clean by construction.** It never contains the weird UCUM unit spellings, the repeated clinical events, or the real missingness a Boston ICU produces over a decade. A tool that scores perfectly on synthetic data has proven it handles the cases its authors imagined.

So we built a realism harness (`fhir4ds.realism`) — corpus-agnostic, deterministic, seeded — around the MIMIC-IV-FHIR demo: the Beth Israel ICU dataset converted to FHIR R4, 100 patients and roughly 900K resources of genuinely messy clinical content. (The harness can also inject seeded duplicate patients for matching experiments, but that is not what this post is about. MIMIC's identities are de-identification artifacts — `Patient_10007795`, birth dates in the 2080s — so it is not a patient-matching corpus and we don't use it as one.)

## Resource deduplication on real repeated clinical events

Deduplication over the full corpus found **46,469 duplicate clusters** — and here is the part synthetic corpora can never give you: **we didn't inject a single one.** These are real repeated clinical events — the same lab drawn and re-reported, the same vitals streamed from the monitor, the same medication administered and documented twice. Every cluster is a genuine redundancy a downstream analyst would otherwise double-count.

Two results mattered most:

- **Zero cross-patient clusters.** The hard safety invariant: not one of the 46,469 clusters spans two patients. Deduplication that merges two people's data is worse than no deduplication.
- **A curation queue with something real in it.** 216,739 near-miss pairs (looks-alike-but-not-merged, routed for review), 7,650 vocabulary-unmatched resources, and 1,065 unresolved medication references — queued for human review rather than silently bucketed. On our synthetic corpora that same queue had always been empty. The code-fallback tier we built for exactly this population had never seen a real member of it until now.

## The auto-coding experiment: two arms on the same slice

We ran the auto-coder two ways against the same 12-patient slice (86,224 resources). The **production path** augments the export exactly as shipped — no codings removed, originals untouched, appends only. The **scored path** strips codings from 1,643 resources first, because that arm needs ground truth and PIQI-as-scored needs text to work from.

### The production path: what auto-coding adds to a real export

The clearest result is **MedicationRequest**. MIMIC ships medications as *name-codes* — the coding's `code` literally is the drug name ("Ondansetron"), under MIMIC's private `mimic-medication-name` system, with no display and no standard vocabulary. Measured on the coding columns, zero of 2,429 medication requests were recognized, displayed, or complete before. The system-aware auto-coder (it skips concepts already coded in standard terminologies, per its INV-4 contract) appended RxNorm codings to 360 of them — the only class where recognition, display, and completeness all move together, and the strongest production story: local drug names become addressable standard terminology.

What auto-coding can and cannot recover falls into a clean three-tier taxonomy:

| tier | population (n) | recognized before → after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| already standard | procedures with SNOMED originals (224 of 494) | recognized as-shipped; auto-coding adds nothing |
| alias-recovered | conditions and procedures under MIMIC's wrapped ICD systems (669 + 121) | 0 → 669 conditions (100%); procedures 224 → 345 via environment aliases |
| translation-required | medications as local name-codes (2,429) | 0 → 0 without help; auto-coding moves 360 to RxNorm |

The **conditions tier is the honest one**: MIMIC's ICD-9/ICD-10 codes sit under `mimic-diagnosis-icd9/icd10` wrapper URIs, so they initially scored as unrecognized. The fix is an *environment alias* — a declarative data table mapping those wrapper URIs to their standard ICD counterparts (ICD-10-CM for diagnoses, ICD-10-PCS for procedures), consulted after the standard-registry spellings. With the aliases registered, every ICD condition is recognized before auto-coding touches anything — so auto-coding's added value there is **not** a recognition score change; it is universal portability (SNOMED equivalents any consumer trusts, no alias table required) and clinical semantics.¹ The proprietary medication systems (`mimic-medication-name`, formulary codes, `poe-iv`) deliberately get no alias: their codes have no standard counterpart, which is exactly the translation-required signal. ²

PIQI **as scored** shows no movement in this arm, and that is itself a finding: the packaged Concept assessments are gated on `code.text` being populated, and MIMIC ships none — so the production-path value lives in the coding columns above, while the strip arm below demonstrates the scored path end to end.

### The scored path: strip and reconstruct

MIMIC's export is fully coded — zero text-only CodeableConcepts in the slice — so we manufactured the legacy situation every EHR migration actually faces: we stripped the codings from 1,643 coded resources (669 conditions, 494 procedures, 480 lab results, keeping the display text as `code.text`), ran the PIQI scorecard, let the auto-coder reconstruct codes from that text, and re-ran the identical scorecard. Because *we* removed the codes, we know exactly what the right answer was — ground-truth auto-coding accuracy on real ICU text, not a vibe.

We ran two configurations: the shipped strict default (only `certain`-grade matches are written) and a recall-oriented arm (`probable` and better).

**The scorecard legs.** On the 12-patient slice (86,224 resources, 98,202 scored items), PIQI as-is scored **38.5** — and that number is itself a finding: the deductions are dominated by genuinely absent lab result values and units, the kind of real-world incompleteness synthetic corpora never model. After stripping, the coding-dependent assessments failed exactly as predicted (`Concept_IsComplete` on all 1,643 stripped elements, plus the lab `Concept_Has*` family). A counterfactual "ceiling" run — the stripped corpus with its original codings quietly re-attached — scored **41.7**, which isolates the 3,083 items that purely represent lost codings.

**Recovery.** The strict arm wrote codings for 28 elements (all conditions, every one a `certain`-grade SNOMED match) and recovered 28 items — right codes, conservative posture, zero new failures. The recall arm wrote 3,366 codings across 1,216 of the 1,643 stripped elements (74% coverage) and recovered **2,188 of the 3,083 fail items — 71% of the coding gap — with zero new failures**: nothing that passed before regressed, including the plausibility checks.

**Ground truth.** This is the number you can only get by removing codes you already have. The stripped corpus's original codings split into two populations: 224 procedures whose originals are **SNOMED** codes, and everything else whose originals are MIMIC-local dictionaries (ICD-9/10 diagnosis codes, local lab item ids). On the standard-terminology subset, the auto-coder recovered the **exact original (system, code) pair for 224 of 224 — 100% top-1 exact recovery**. On the local-dictionary subset, exact match is impossible by construction — the engine doesn't know MIMIC's private dictionaries — and instead it returns standard SNOMED/LOINC equivalents. Spot-checking those: "Anemia in chronic kidney disease" (ICD-9 local) → SNOMED 707323002 *Anemia in chronic kidney disease*; "Portal vein thrombosis" → SNOMED 17920008 *Portal vein thrombosis*. That is precisely the EHR-migration promise: legacy local codes become addressable standard terminology, with the original text preserved and every generated coding carrying machine-readable provenance (engine, version, search mode, score, match grade, `userSelected=false`).

**Does the retrieval mode matter?** We ablated the terminology engine's three search modes on the identical slice (same strip, same gold):

| mode | PIQI index after auto-coding | elements re-coded | top-1 exact recovery (standard-terminology subset) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lexical only | 40.24 | 48% | 11.2% |
| semantic only | 40.80 | 78% | 2.4% |
| hybrid | 40.76 | 75% | 13.6% |

The trade is clean. Pure semantic search maximizes *coverage* — it finds a plausible standard concept for almost everything — but its exact-code recovery collapses: embeddings rank paraphrases highly, and "a reasonable concept" is not "the right code." Pure lexical search is the opposite: strong on exact matches (the surface-form tiebreak anchors verbatim text to its code) but finds codes for only half the corpus. Hybrid delivers semantic-grade coverage *with* lexical-grade exactness — the best top-1 exact recovery of the three at near-best coverage, which is why it is the engine's default and ours.

¹ The alias table ships as data in the PIQI code-system registry, so a different export environment registers its own wrappers without code changes; recognition through an alias is distinguishable from a standard spelling in the verdict's provenance.

² Procedures are the sparsest tier: auto-coding reached 187 of 494, and after the run 418 of 494 (85%) are recognized — SNOMED originals, alias-recovered ICD, and appended codings together. An earlier no-strip run under-counted this class because ICD-10-PCS was missing from the standard code-system registry, so appended PCS codings scored as unrecognized until we closed that gap.

## Why this matters more than another synthetic suite

Three things made this exercise worth more than another constructed test suite:

1. **Real data exercises vocabulary and shape you didn't author.** MIMIC's UCUM annotations, local code dictionaries, and export quirks (concepts that carry coding displays but never `code.text`) are the populations our synthetic corpora never modeled — and they change what the tools actually do.
2. **Real redundancy needs no injection.** 46,469 genuine duplicate clusters with zero cross-patient merges is a validation synthetic duplicates can approximate but never earn.
3. **The gaps you find are the honest ones.** Measured recovery rates against known-truth codes — including where the auto-coder can't reach — are an accurate self-portrait. "Perfect on synthetic" alone is marketing.

Both experiments ship in fhir4ds: the realism harness (`fhir4ds.realism`) works on any FHIR NDJSON or bundle corpus, and the PIQI auto-coding benchmark (`fhir4ds.realism.piqi_autocoding`) is deterministic and repeatable. Point them at your own exports — the interesting bugs are the ones your test data doesn't contain.
